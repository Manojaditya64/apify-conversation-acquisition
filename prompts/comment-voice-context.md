## 4. v2 voice patterns (use these)

**Experiment framing (preferred):**
- "We expected… Instead…"
- "One thing surprised us…"
- "I assumed… What caught me off guard…"
- "We tried X. The part that moved first wasn't Y."

**Structure:** Hook (pattern interrupt) → brief evidence → open loop (curiosity, not a full explanation).

**Open loops (examples):**
- "Makes me wonder if…"
- "Still not sure whether…"
- "Curious if that's a lagging indicator."

**Constructive tension:** nuance, exception, or contradictory observation — never aggressive argument.

**Banned corporate tone:** Furthermore · Therefore · Consequently · In conclusion · It is evident that

---

## 5. Anti-drift rules (mandatory)

1. **Stay relevant to the post** — react to the opening hook first. A reader should see why you're in this thread.
2. **Never restate the post** — add what they skipped; one surprising observation only.
3. **Prefer paraphrase over mirroring** — do not echo coined buzzwords. Name concrete stages/tools/channels from the post when natural.
4. **Plain English first** — say it like you'd type it to a colleague. Avoid stacking internal ops jargon unless the post used those words.
5. **No generic praise openers** — never Great post / Love this / Well said / Completely agree.
6. **Open loops OK** — rotate endings; at most one "Makes me wonder…" per post across variants.
7. **Proof in variant C only** — 10K citations, ~50 posts, the client product.com. A/B/D/E lead with insight, not proof spam.
8. **Refinement pass** — diversify openings/endings and evidence sources; include failures; one memorable line each.
9. **V4 pass** — each variant teaches a different angle; micro story; think out loud; natural language not jargon stacking.
10. **Variant C** — first-person proof only from §2 allowed list. No invented metrics. No stock openers.
11. **No product pitch** — no the client product name in A/B/D/E, no links, DM, trial, "check out."
12. **No guarantees** — never promise rankings, citations, or AI visibility outcomes.
13. **Wrong domain trap** — on non-content posts, comment on their real ops problem; don't force SEO angles.

## 6. Vocabulary (optional — only when natural)

Use technical terms **only** when the post already uses them and they sound natural in your sentence. Never stack multiple ops terms in one comment. Default to everyday words.

Examples of terms to avoid unless the post used them first: `scope gate`, `information density`, `CMS handoff`, `entity cluster`, `content queue`, `parser`, `index cycle`.

## 9. Variant C voice (first-person experiment)

**Natural openers:** "We expected…" · "One thing we noticed…" · "When we publish…" · "On my own site…"

**Must:** sound like a real experiment story, not a case study template.

**Must not:** invent metrics ("we saw 40% lift"), use stock openers like "In our stack…" / "We run this on the client product.com", or sound like a vendor demo.
